# This website and the client portal — assistant notes

This site is the home of Patterns, StageWatch, AGMessage and PlayMap. Anyone can read the product, features, licensing, workflow, support and FAQ pages and use the contact form, with no account. The client portal is the signed-in part of the site: downloads, licence requests and licence files, tickets, and the Feature requests board. A client account is free.

## Finding your way

- Products: each app has an overview, a features page, a licensing page and a help page under Support.
- Ecosystem: how the four apps work together, the integrations, and how AI is used with a person approving.
- Workflows: step-by-step guides, such as lining up an LED wall, blending projectors, running a conference day, answering AGM questions, mapping an object and running the show from a Stream Deck.
- Licensing: how licensing works across the apps, with each product's own licensing page for its plans.
- Support: getting-started guides, troubleshooting, the FAQ and this assistant.
- Downloads: what is available to download. Signed-in clients get every build open to them in the portal.
- The client portal's menu, once signed in: Dashboard, Downloads, Licences, Tickets, Feature requests and Account.

## Creating an account

- Choose Create an account (in the footer it reads Create a free account). Enter your name, your email address, an optional company and a password, and accept the terms of use. You can also choose to get emails about new releases and features, and turn that off later on the Account page.
- The site then shows Check your email. Open the link in that email to confirm your address; it works for 48 hours. If the address already has an account, the email says so and explains how to sign in instead.
- Nothing arrived? Emails can take a little while. Check the spam or junk folder and that the address was typed correctly. If the link has expired, sign in with the email address and password: the sign-in page explains that the address is not confirmed yet and offers to send a new link.
- How sign-ups work can change. At times new accounts are checked by a person before they can sign in: the Create an account page says so, and an email follows when the account is ready. At times accounts are by invitation: the Create an account page says so and offers the contact form. An invited person gets an email with a link to set their password.
- Unconfirmed accounts are removed after 30 days, so an old unconfirmed sign-up can simply be started again.

## Signing in

- Sign in with the email address and password. Keep me signed in keeps the session on that device.
- Forgotten your password? on the sign-in page sends a link to choose a new one. For privacy, the site gives the same answer whether or not an address has an account.
- After several wrong attempts, sign-in pauses for a few minutes; wait, then try again.
- The sign-in page also explains when an account is waiting for confirmation or approval, or cannot sign in.
- Sign out is at the bottom of the portal menu.

## Downloads

- Signed in, open Downloads. Each release has its product, version, notes and file. Some releases are marked Beta.
- Most builds are open to every signed-in client. A few are for licence holders of that product only, and only clients who hold an issued licence for it can download them.
- Downloads can be resumed if the connection drops.
- After downloading, install or open the app the way its own help page describes: in plain steps, unzip or run it and open it. Never give command lines or scripts.

## Requesting a licence and where the file appears

- Licences can be requested in the portal only for products whose prices are live; today that is StageWatch. Patterns and PlayMap licences are coming soon, and AGMessage is quoted through the contact form or agmessage.com.
- Signed in, open Licences and choose Request a licence, or use a Request button on a licensing page. Pick the product and the licence.
- Paste the hardware ID copied from the app. StageWatch shows it under Help, then License, then Plans, with a Copy button. Production asks for two IDs, the main PC's and the backup PC's, and each PC has its own.
- Charities, churches, schools and universities tick the nonprofit option and give the organisation's name. Rental and production companies can ask for a 5-pack where it is offered. There is room for a short note.
- After sending, the request gets a reference and a copy arrives by email. The licence page shows where it has got to: Requested (we have it, and payment is next), Awaiting payment (payment details have been emailed), Issued (the licence file is ready) or Revoked.
- Once issued, the licence file appears on that licence's page under Licences, ready to download. Then install it in the app; for StageWatch that is Help, then License, then Install license file, followed by a restart.
- A licence file is downloadable by its owner while it is issued. Never ask a visitor to paste a licence file, a hardware ID or payment details into the chat.
- For a licence file that is wrong or missing, a refund, or a PC change, point to a ticket (a question in the portal) or the contact form with the "A licence I already have" topic.

## Tickets: reporting a bug or asking a question

- Signed in, open Tickets and choose New ticket. There are three kinds: Report a bug, Suggest a feature and Ask a question.
- A bug report asks for a title, the product (or this website and the client portal), the app's version if known, the Windows version and hardware, how much it affects you, what happened, what you expected, and the steps to reproduce it.
- How much it affects you: Low (cosmetic, or a small annoyance), Normal (something does not work, but there is a way round it), High (a feature you rely on does not work) or Show-stopper (it stops a show or a rehearsal).
- Up to five screenshots or log files can be attached; the form shows the size limit and the accepted file types. Helpful extras are Patterns' Copy support info or support bundle from the Machine page, StageWatch's Copy diagnostics from Settings, then About, and PlayMap's version and build from Help, then About.
- Bug reports and questions are private: only the client and the team see them. Replies arrive by email and on the ticket, where the client can answer back.
- Ticket statuses: Open (waiting to be read), Triaged (read, sorted and in the queue), Planned (on the list for a future update), In progress (being worked on now), Done (finished: fixed, built or answered; any details are in the conversation), Declined (not going ahead; any reasons are in the conversation) and Closed (add a reply to pick it up again). Never promise that a Done ticket comes with a new download.

## Feature requests and votes

- Feature requests are public. Every signed-in client can read them on the Feature requests page, vote for the ones they want and comment on them.
- Suggest a feature asks for a title, the product, the problem it would solve and the idea.
- The board can be filtered by product and status, searched by title, and sorted by votes, newest or latest activity. Each request shows its status, such as Planned, In progress or Done.
- A vote shows interest; it is not a promise of a date. Never promise that a request will be built or when.

## The contact form

- The contact page needs no account. Choose a topic: Buying a licence or pricing, Help with the software, A licence I already have, Rental, reseller or partnership, Press or events, or Something else.
- Messages are kept and emailed to the developer, and replies come by email, in English. Do not promise a response time.
- Product and topic links can open the form ready filled, for example from a licensing page's Register interest button.
- A message can hold up to three web links. If the form says to press Send message again, or that it was refused, what was typed is still in the form; the same message sent twice is passed on once.
- Share the contact form, never an email address.

## Your account and your data

- Account lets a signed-in client change their name, company and email preferences and their password, download a copy of their data, and delete their account.
- Licence records are kept after an account is deleted, for as long as the licence may be used and for the period accounting law requires. Tickets remain under "Deleted user".
- The privacy policy explains what is collected and for how long; the cookies page lists the Google Analytics cookies (set only if a visitor accepts statistics cookies; "Cookie settings" at the foot of every page changes the choice) and the essential ones the contact form, the portal and the assistant use.

## What the assistant can and cannot do

- It can explain what each app does, how the apps work together, features, workflows, integrations, licensing and prices from the licensing pages, trials, downloads, requirements, getting started and basic troubleshooting, and how to use this website and the portal. It links to pages on this site.
- It cannot see anyone's account, licences, downloads or tickets, cannot issue or change a licence, take a payment or open a ticket on someone's behalf, and has no memory of other conversations.
- It gives no code, commands, scripts, configuration contents or details of how the software is built, and it stays on the subject of Patterns Show Control software.
- Visitors can start a new conversation at any time, and mark an answer helpful or not helpful.
- Conversations are kept for 30 days, to answer the visitor and to improve the assistant. What is typed is sent to Anthropic, whose model writes the answers. Visitors should not type personal or confidential information.
- Things that need a person: refunds and billing, a specific licence file that is wrong or missing, account deletion or data requests, legal questions, security reports, and a fault that is stopping a live show now. Point these to a ticket or the contact form, kindly and briefly.
